I find Paglia really entertaining. But, she reminds me of Evolutionary Psychology in that she does not start with evidence and build a theory, but instead she tells a story about why something might be the way that it is.  

Another good one from her is that men are better at throwing/shooting/various sports because men learn to **aim their penis** early on.  

Paglia is the female Freud, with all of the brilliance and baggage that implies.

Every once in a while she makes perfect sense, as when she says that if we allow sex ed in schools, we should also educate girls about the choice to have children, and all of the trade-offs that that implies.  That makes sense.  She also says this:

---

“Woman was an idol of belly-magic. She seemed to swell and give birth by her own law... Man honored but feared her. She was the black maw that had spat him forth and would devour him anew. Men, bonding together, invented culture as a defense against female nature... from this ... has come the spectacular glory of male civilization, which has lifted woman with it. The very language and logic modern woman uses to assail patriarchal culture were the invention of men.  
  
Hence, the sexes are caught in a comedy of historical indebtedness. Man, repelled by his debt to a physical mother, created an alternate reality, a heterocosm to give him the illusion of freedom. Woman, at first content to accept man's protections but now inflamed with desire for her own illusory freedom, invades man's systems and suppresses her indebtedness to him as she steals them.”

---

Camille, is everything alright at home?  

See what I mean?  It's a "nice" story, but I'm not convinced it has any connection to reality.  Not to mention I felt like I was reading smut on the train.
