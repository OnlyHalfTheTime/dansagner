## Imagine 1984.  This is different, but still same.

We had to read both BNW and 1984 in High School, and I always liked Huxley's portentous prose more.  Sure yeah, it's a dystopian novel.  But it takes dystopia to utopian places and asks that the reader be careful what he wishes for.

If 1984 shows us what totalitarian socialism looks like, BNW shows us what Fully-Automated-Luxury-Gay-Space-Communism looks like.  Apparently, it looks like young children having orgies in bushes while the adults look on.  If that didn't just squick you out, don't worry.  They also breed sub-human anthropoids to act as their willing slaves.  They are willing because they were bred to be willing.

You see, BNW answers the question: Since Communism is seemingly incompatible with man's nature, what if we could control that nature?  We don't here take the route of Pol Pot or Mao, killing those who show any sign of intelligence.  We don't take the path of Gramsci, slowly taking of the means of educating the people.  We take the road of the Lenin, combining science with theory.  We control the individual from conception, decanting them in bottles instead of a woman carrying to term.  There are no mothers, no fathers, just adults and children.  Even there, the lines are blurred.

Ever-present hypnotic suggestions and thought-terminating cliches keep the populace placid, and moving in the right direction.  When that fails, there's always drugs: Soma.  A gram is better than a damn.  You can take a little to relax, or a lot to fast-forward through the next few days.  Despite this, society keeps functioning.  It's almost as unbelievable as *Anthem*.

I do suggest this book, if only for a glimpse at a progenitor to kawaii-noir.  Like most good literature, it's not for the faint of heart.
