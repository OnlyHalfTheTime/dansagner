This is the book that started my obsession and completionist jaunt down the rabbit hole that is Sanderson's Cosmere.  My brother does this.  He finds something and says "Hey, you might like this." then we cut to months later and see me obsession over it still while he's lost interest.  Satisfactory, Deep Rock Galactic, now the Cosmere.  It's a trade-off.  On the one hand, I'd rather we dork out together, but on the other hand, he has introduced me to some of my favorite entertainment, and I've learned to value his recommendations.  So, thanks, Bill.

The first entry in the Mistborn series is prototypical fantasy.  As in: if you wish to write fantasy, pick up this book and see how it's done.  It's an easy read, and there is no wasted time.  The tropes are there, of course.  An orphaned and beaten street urchin is secretly a wizard?  Color me flabbergasted.  However, just like you can walk the same road a hundred times and still notice something new, Sanderson manages to turn a familiar genesis into something novel.

As always, Sanderson's magic system is spell-binding (pun intended).  Allomancy is probably his best magic system to date, and he only piles on top of it with two more systems.  They way they each mingle and work with/against each other is fascinating.  If you like that kind of fantasy, it's great.  If you don't, maybe skip BranSan entirely.  I'm here for the cool powers.

As a springboard into the Cosmere, I could not recommend a better start.  You'll get a good sense of whether or not you want to continue.
